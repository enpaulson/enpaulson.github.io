About Me

Research & teaching

I am an Assistant Professor at Hanyang University, South Korea, specializing in wireless communications, LEO satellite networks, distributed optimization, and AI-enabled intelligent sensing. My research explores resource-efficient communication and computing systems, including intelligent network coordination, localization, and sensing. Alongside research in the Department of Electronic Engineering, I contribute to undergraduate and graduate teaching across engineering, mathematics, computational thinking, and interdisciplinary design.

01 / Research area

Satellite Networks

Moving LEO satellites couple changing coverage with limited onboard resources, overlapping beams, and heterogeneous traffic. My work addresses beam–user alignment and sub-channel assignment through iterative matching, explicitly linking beam selection to interference and loading. For beam-hopping systems, user clustering and decomposed time–frequency, power, and dynamic resource allocation coordinate downlink service under mixed channel conditions. Complementary work derives theoretical limits for estimating task-offloading delay and energy under intermittent space–ground connectivity. Together, these studies connect practical scheduling and resource coordination with an understanding of the uncertainty imposed by finite observations and changing link availability.

02 / Research area

Distributed Optimization

Dynamic space–ground networks require decisions that remain feasible when global information is incomplete and links change rapidly. My research develops distributed, two-timescale online optimization for task offloading: fast queue-aware updates coordinate resources using local measurements and intermittent neighbor information, while slower updates adapt satellite service placement. Analysis characterizes queue stability, cost–backlog trade-offs, and dynamic regret for the relaxed placement update. Related work formulates distributed controller placement in LEO-assisted software-defined integrated satellite–terrestrial networks, combining delay-constrained clustering with multiobjective optimization. These approaches examine how signaling latency, processing overhead, and changing connectivity shape scalable coordination without continuous global synchronization.

03 / Research area

AI & Intelligent Sensing

Wireless measurements contain useful information about people and location, but noise, incomplete fingerprints, and environmental variation complicate inference. My work investigates learning-based methods that extract this information from practical sensing and localization data. An IR-UWB study uses neural classification for joint vital-sign and occupancy detection, comparing competing classifiers across experimental scenarios. Indoor localization studies investigate hybrid quantum–deep neural architectures and dropout autoencoder augmentation of incomplete Wi-Fi FTM–RSS fingerprints, followed by neural regression. The emphasis is on measurement-aware learning and experimental evaluation: reconstructing missing signal features and relating learned representations to sensing or positioning tasks, rather than assuming ideal input data.

04 / Research area

Positioning & Navigation Systems

Reliable positioning depends on both the information in radio measurements and the selection of useful infrastructure. My research studies Wi-Fi FTM fingerprinting, FTM/RSSI fusion, and context-aware access-point selection for neural indoor localization, using office measurements and smartphone-compatible technologies. In cell-free massive MIMO, radio-stripe selection examines how infrastructure subsets affect user position estimation. Complementary collaborative work on LEO positioning, navigation, and timing derives performance bounds for carrier-phase positioning, considering information from Doppler and carrier phase. These studies connect data-driven indoor estimators with analytical accuracy limits, clarifying how measurement quality, infrastructure selection, and signal observables influence achievable positioning performance.

05 / Research area

Cognitive Radio & Software-Defined Networks

Efficient spectrum use and programmable control require evidence about radio activity and network behavior. My cognitive-radio research measures spectrum occupancy in Lagos and evaluates interference temperature and occupancy in measurement campaigns, examining how observed activity and detection thresholds inform opportunities for dynamic spectrum access. Related software-defined networking studies evaluate latency, jitter, and throughput, including quality of service in software-defined IoT architectures. These investigations combine measurement-based spectrum characterization with simulation-based network evaluation. Their contributions concern the conditions under which spectrum appears available and how separating network control from forwarding affects service performance, providing a practical basis for assessing cognitive and programmable networks.

Teaching

Teaching Philosophy

My teaching approach emphasizes a strong foundation in engineering principles, analytical reasoning, and the practical application of theoretical knowledge. I aim to help students develop the ability to understand complex concepts, formulate engineering problems, and evaluate solutions systematically. Through structured explanations, worked examples, interactive discussions, and application-oriented activities, I encourage active participation and independent thinking. I also emphasize the connection between classroom learning and real-world engineering challenges, particularly in communications, computing, and emerging technologies. My goal is to cultivate students' technical competence, critical thinking, and confidence in solving engineering problems.
